# Household-Recovery-Measurement-Database
Household recovery after disasters is a complex process influenced by various factors. By compiling research on household recovery, this work seeks to support researchers and practitioners in identifying effective approaches to measuring and promoting household resilience. The Household Recovery Measurement Database compiles categorical and context specific information for each of the studies reviewed in NIST TN 2389 - A Review of Measurement Methods for the Recovery of Households After Disasters: An Annotated Bibliography. The database can be searched and filtered by relevant keywords and categories, providing a valuable resource for researchers and practitioners seeking to identify relevant studies, compare methodologies, and access key findings related to household recovery after disasters.

Data-Specific Information:
The following are descriptions of each of the categories within the Household Recovery Data Set. Each row is associated with one study reviewed in NIST TN 2389, and each column is associated with a single category. To view the complete reviews for each study and for more information and specific details regarding methodology of the bibliography, please view the full publication of NIST TN 2389 here: https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/TechnicalNotes/NIST.TN.2389.pdf

Bibliography Number: The number associated with where the review of a specific study exists within the written bibliography (NIST TN 2389).
Study Title: The title of the article reviewed.
Study Purpose: The objectives or goals of the study.
Sample Size: The number of households or other relevant participants sampled in the study.
Sampling Characteristics: Information on the strategy and the types of people sampled.
Disaster: A single-word description of the disaster type affecting the businesses (e.g., Hurricane, Earthquake, Flood, Drought).
Built Environment: Indicates whether aspects of the built environment were included in the recovery measurement (Yes/No).
Timeframe: The period after the disaster during which household recovery was assessed.
Time Point Measured: The specific point(s) in time when data was collected.
Measurement of Recovery: The specific approach or metric(s) used to evaluate household recovery.
Data Method: The method of data collection used in the study.
Instrument Included: Whether the study included the specific measurement instruments or a functional description (could be used for reproduction) of instrument items used to gather data (Yes/No/Partially).
Methodology: The specific research methods and/or analyses employed in the study.
Validation: The steps taken to verify or validate the measurement approach or model.
